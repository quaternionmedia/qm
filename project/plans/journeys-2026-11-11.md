# Journeys: window sets and a rail of steps: a plan

*A plan, dated. Built: slices 1 and 2, each one commit of `build/journeys`, and
slice 3 as the chain the tour takes ([docs/viewer.md](../../../../docs/viewer.md#journeys),
[docs/tour.md](../../../../docs/tour.md)): seven journeys beside the first, named for what the page
has rather than as the family's table had them.*

## What this plans

A path a person takes through the page passes through windows in an order: a part
chosen in Site, its numbers in Parameters, Slice in a machine's Print window, the
file's path drawn in the world, Print. The page has every window, and Views that lay
several out together (⊞ Windows › Views: Default, Part, Machine, Airgap), but nothing
that holds the path itself: which windows go together for it, which step a person is
on, and what comes next. The family's journeys
([family-journeys-2026-11-07.md](family-journeys-2026-11-07.md), *The journeys*) name
the paths; this plan gives the page two things to walk them by:

- **a window set for a journey**: the windows it passes through laid out together, a
  View like the others, chosen from ⊞ Windows;
- **a rail of its steps**: numbered in the header while the journey is entered, the
  step a person is on filled, and **Next**, which names the step after it. A step
  brings its windows forward.

## Where the rail comes from

codecartographer's header has it (`web/src/layout/components/step_rail.ts`,
`web/src/layout/steps.ts`, and its `docs/ui.md`, *The steps*), taken from alfred's
stage rail (Source -> Edit -> Render -> Deliver, alfred's `website/src/Stages.js`).
What is taken, as codecartographer has it:

- the rail is an ordered list of buttons, the step a person is on marked
  `aria-current="step"`, each numbered, then **Next** with the step after's number
  and name, which a narrow window shows as its number alone;
- a step **owns** windows and brings some **forward**: entering it puts those in
  front of their stacks and opens one closed since, the step already on included;
- **choosing a window moves the rail** to the step that owns it -- its tab, ⊞
  Windows or a stack's + -- and moves nothing else.

What differs, and why: a step here is entered on a site whose pieces are already
drawn, so a reload can land where a person left off, as alfred's does: the
journey and its step are in the address (`?journey=make&step=slice`), beside the
site, the focus and the machine it already holds. And the page's world is never
hidden by a step: codecartographer's last step maximises its canvas, but every
step here acts on what the world shows.

## The first journey: Print a part on a machine

Its window set is the one the integration walk's third stop arrived at, by hand, to
slice the footpedal for printer_1: the world beside a column of Site (with
Problems, Pinned, Jobs and SCAD behind it), Selected, Parameters, and a stack of
the chosen machine's window and its Print window (with Pictures, Bench and
Settings behind them).

| | Step | Brings forward | Owns |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Choose** | Site, then Selected | Site, Problems, Pinned, SCAD |
| 2 | **Shape** | Parameters, then Selected | Selected, Parameters |
| 3 | **Slice** | the machine's Print window | the machine's Print window |
| 4 | **See** | the machine's Print window, its file's path drawn in the world | -- |
| 5 | **Print** | the machine's window, then Jobs | the machine's window, Jobs |

A machine's windows are there only while its Machine is open: a step that brings
one forward with none open opens the chosen piece's machine, as the Machine view
does, and says so where no piece names one.

## The slices

1. **The window set.** `apothecary/journeys.py` holds *Print*'s view, laid out as
   above, given to the page whole as the systems are and defined there by
   `static/systems.js` after theirs: listed in ⊞ Windows › Views and on the ring's
   Panels › Windows, wearing the Machine group's colour; with no Machine open it
   opens the selected machine's, as the Machine view does. Its browser test lays it
   out from ⊞ Windows with printer_1's Machine open and the footpedal chosen and
   holds each window in its stack.
2. **The journeys and the rail.** `apothecary/journeys.py` gains each journey's
   steps (what each owns and brings forward), and a unit test holds every window it
   names to one the page registers, each owned by one step of its journey. The header's rail, entered from
   ⊞ Windows › Journeys and left by its ✕, numbered, Next, a step entered bringing
   its windows forward, a window chosen moving the rail, the address holding the
   journey and its step. Its browser test walks *Print a part* from Choose to Start
   on the demo's footpedal and printer_1, Next by Next, and back by a window's tab.
3. **The other journeys**: *Start a site*, *Flash a board*, *A picture
   to a piece* and *Design a part*, each its window set and its steps, from the
   family's journeys table.

## Not planned here

A step that does its window's work for a person -- Slice pressed, the preview turned
on -- rather than bringing the window where it is done. The census
(`apothecary census`) reads the rail as one listener, a widget that changes what is
seen; the page's count of its own controls stays where it was.
